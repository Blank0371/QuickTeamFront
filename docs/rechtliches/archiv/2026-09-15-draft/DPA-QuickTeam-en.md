## Section 1 — Subject matter, duration and specification of the processing

(1) The subject matter of this agreement is the processing of personal data by the Processor on behalf of the Controller in connection with the provision and operation of the Software-as-a-Service application "QuickTeam" (the "Service"). The basis is the usage contract concluded between the Parties (the Terms).

(2) The nature and purpose of the processing, the type of personal data processed, and the categories of data subjects are set out exhaustively in **Annex 1**.

(3) The duration of this agreement corresponds to the term of the usage contract. The obligations under this agreement continue beyond termination of the usage contract for as long as the Processor processes or stores the Controller's personal data.

(4) The primary database and its backups are stored with Supabase in the Ireland region. This does not promise that all processing occurs exclusively in the EU: web delivery, server-side web processing, email and push delivery, and support access may involve third countries as set out in Annex 3. Transfers require a valid basis under Articles 44 et seq. GDPR. Additional sub-processors and changes are subject to Section 6.

(5) This agreement is concluded electronically: the person acting for the Customer accepts it at registration or, for a new version, in the dashboard, and in doing so confirms that they are authorised to represent the Customer. For each acceptance, the Processor stores the version of this agreement, the time, the acting account and the Customer. On request, it provides the Controller with these details and with the text of the agreement in the accepted version in text form.

## Section 2 — Controller's right to issue instructions

(1) The Processor processes personal data only on documented instructions from the Controller, unless required to process by Union or Member State law to which it is subject. In such a case, the Processor informs the Controller of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.

(2) Instructions are initially determined and specified by this agreement and the usage contract, and by the use of the Service's functions by the Controller and its Users. Individual instructions must be issued in text form (e.g. by email to blanktrading@web.de).

(3) If the Processor considers an instruction unlawful under data-protection law, it informs the Controller without undue delay and suspends the processing concerned until the legal issue is resolved. Mere confirmation of an unlawful instruction does not make its execution lawful.

## Section 3 — Obligations of the Processor

(1) The Processor processes the data in accordance with the GDPR and this agreement. It assists the Controller in complying with data-protection provisions as set out below.

(2) The Processor ensures that persons authorized to process the data have committed themselves to confidentiality or are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality (Art. 28(3)(b), Art. 29, Art. 32(4) GDPR).

(3) The Processor takes the technical and organizational measures required under Art. 32 GDPR. These are described in **Annex 2**. The Processor may further develop and adapt the measures during the term of the agreement, provided the agreed level of protection is not reduced.

(4) Management handles data-protection enquiries at blanktrading@web.de. No data protection officer is currently appointed. The Provider reviews the appointment requirements under Article 37 GDPR and Section 38 BDSG against its actual activities, risks and size; employing fewer than twenty persons in processing does not by itself exclude an obligation.

(5) The Processor rectifies, erases or restricts the processing of the data only on the Controller's instructions. Where a data subject contacts the Processor directly, the Processor forwards the request to the Controller without undue delay.

## Section 4 — Assistance to the Controller

(1) The Processor assists the Controller by appropriate technical and organizational measures, insofar as possible, in fulfilling its obligation to respond to requests from data subjects exercising their rights under Chapter III of the GDPR (Art. 12 to 23 GDPR).

(2) The Processor further assists the Controller in complying with the obligations under Art. 32 to 36 GDPR (security of processing, notification and communication of personal-data breaches, data-protection impact assessment, prior consultation), taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to it.

(3) Additional fees require a prior agreement for separately commissioned services. They must not obstruct statutory assistance duties or the exercise of audit or data-subject rights. The free-of-charge provisions in the Terms apply to export and switching.

## Section 5 — Notification of personal-data breaches

(1) The Processor notifies the Controller of any personal-data breach affecting the Controller's data of which it becomes aware, without undue delay after becoming aware of it.

(2) The notification contains, where possible, the information referred to in Art. 33(3) GDPR. The Processor takes, in coordination with the Controller, appropriate measures to secure the data and to mitigate possible adverse effects. The obligations to notify the supervisory authority and to communicate to data subjects under Art. 33, 34 GDPR lie with the Controller.

## Section 6 — Sub-processors

(1) The Controller grants the Processor general authorization to engage further processors (sub-processors). The sub-processors engaged at the time of conclusion are listed in **Annex 3** and are approved by the Controller.

(2) The Processor informs the Controller of any intended change concerning the addition or replacement of sub-processors in text form with reasonable prior notice. The Controller may object to a change on important data-protection grounds within fourteen (14) days. In the event of a justified objection, the Parties will endeavor to reach an amicable solution; if none is reached, the Controller is entitled to terminate the usage contract for good cause.

(3) The Processor binds sub-processors by contract to data-protection obligations equivalent to those under this agreement, in particular to sufficient guarantees of appropriate technical and organizational measures (Art. 28(4) GDPR). Where a sub-processor fails to fulfill its data-protection obligations, the Processor remains liable to the Controller for the performance of that sub-processor's obligations.

(4) Ancillary services that the Processor procures as third-party services (e.g. telecommunications or pure maintenance services without specific reference to the data processed for the Controller) do not constitute sub-processing within the meaning of this Section. The Processor is nevertheless obliged to take appropriate precautions to ensure data protection and data security in that respect as well.

## Section 7 — Controller's audit rights and evidence

(1) The Processor makes available to the Controller all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with the obligations laid down in Art. 28 GDPR and allows for and contributes to audits, including inspections, conducted by the Controller or an auditor mandated by it.

(2) Evidence may also be provided by presenting suitable, up-to-date certifications, approved codes of conduct, or reports of independent bodies (e.g. audit reports, IT-security attestations).

(3) Audits are normally organised with reasonable advance notice while protecting security, confidentiality and other customers’ rights. Incident-related, urgent and legally required audits remain possible at any time; there is no fixed annual limit. If the proposed auditor has a justified conflict of interest, the Parties agree an independent alternative without excluding the audit right.

## Section 8 — Deletion and return after completion of processing

(1) After services end, at the Controller’s choice the Processor returns the personal data and deletes existing copies, or deletes it without return, unless statutory retention is required. An instruction for earlier deletion remains possible.

(2) The periods in Section 6(4) to (7) of the Terms also apply to this agreement for delivery, timely export requests and switching. The data concerned is not deleted before any ongoing transition, complete delivery and the subsequent retrieval period of at least thirty days are complete. Technical or organisational deletion holds implement these periods; they do not extend productive use without a contractual basis.

(3) Remaining database backup copies expire no later than seven days after deletion from the live system. Until then, they are protected and retained solely for recovery; if a backup is restored, deletions instructed in the meantime are applied again. Deletion is confirmed in text form on request.

(4) The Provider’s own billing records and contract evidence are outside this deletion instruction only insofar as they may lawfully be retained for a specific purpose on the Provider’s own legal basis. Scope and periods are set out in Sections 5.2 and 15 of the Privacy Policy. This does not justify further use of other employee data.

## Section 9 — Liability

Art. 82 GDPR applies to the liability of the Parties. In the internal relationship, the liability provisions of the usage contract (Section 10 of the Terms) additionally apply, insofar as they are compatible with the mandatory requirements of the GDPR.

## Section 10 — Final provisions

(1) In the event of conflicts between this agreement and the usage contract, the provisions of this agreement prevail on data-protection matters.

(2) Individual agreements take precedence (Section 305b BGB). Otherwise, amendments including the annexes require text form; changes to sub-processors are governed by Section 6. Publication alone does not replace any required contractual acceptance.

(3) Should individual provisions of this agreement be or become invalid, the validity of the remaining provisions remains unaffected. The Parties replace the invalid provision with the statutory provision.

(4) The law of the Federal Republic of Germany applies.

## Annex 1 — Description of the processing

**Subject matter:** provision and operation of the SaaS application QuickTeam for staff and shift scheduling for the Controller.

**Nature of processing:** collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission within the Controller's business, making available, alignment, restriction, erasure and destruction of personal data by automated means.

**Purpose of processing:** shift and staff scheduling, management of availability, preferences, vacation and absences, shift swaps, emergency cover, in-team communication (announcements, polls, checklists), sending of notifications (push), technical provision of business access. General administration of personal login accounts, contract evidence and billing is performed under the Provider’s own responsibility as described in the Privacy Policy and is outside this processing instruction. Delivery of business messages on the Customer’s behalf, including the push processing required for that purpose, is covered by this instruction.

**Type of personal data:**
- Contact data: name (first and last name), email address, optionally phone number;
- Profile and contract data: role(s), contract type, target/maximum working time, overtime balance, vacation entitlement, business membership;
- Scheduling and operational data: shift assignments and history, availability, shift preferences, vacation and absence requests, shift-swap and emergency records;
- Communication data: messages, announcements, poll votes, checklist/task status, shift notes;
- Usage and technical data: session identifiers, timestamps, device push tokens, log data.

**Categories of data subjects:** the Controller's staff (including temporary staff and, where recorded, applicants), managers/administrators of the business, and other Users invited by the Controller.

**Special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR):** The Service is not intended for diagnoses, certificates of incapacity for work or similar health records. Free text may nevertheless contain health information. Processing then requires a valid exception under Article 9(2) GDPR in the specific case and appropriate safeguards; acceptance of this DPA does not create such an exception. Unnecessary information must be removed. The Controller and Processor each fulfil their own statutory obligations. Current visibility of emergency reasons is described in Sections 7.4 and 8 of the Privacy Policy; that description does not authorise unnecessary disclosures.

## Annex 2 — Technical and organizational measures (TOMs) under Art. 32 GDPR

> The following measures describe the status at the time of conclusion. Some infrastructure-related measures (physical data-center security, network security, encryption at rest) are provided by the sub-processor Supabase (see Annex 3).

**1. Confidentiality (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)**
- *Physical access control:* primary database in the EU (Ireland); physical access protection by the infrastructure operators. Annex 3 governs other processing and third-country access.
- *System access control:* individual user accounts with authentication (email address and password; email address confirmed by one-time code); passwords stored only as hashes; administrative access (including to the database and management console) is additionally protected by two-factor authentication (2FA).
- *Data access control:* row-level security on all data tables isolating each business's data; server-enforced authorization via secured database functions; least-privilege access.
- *Separation control:* logical multi-tenant separation of data per business.

**2. Integrity (Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR)**
- *Transmission control:* encryption of data transmission according to the state of the art (TLS).
- *Input control:* logging of security-relevant events and timestamps; traceability of changes within the application logic.

**3. Availability and resilience (Art. 32(1)(b), (c) GDPR)**
- encryption at rest and automatic daily database backups (excluding separately stored attachment files, which require separate backup measures) by the hosting sub-processor, with backups retained for seven (7) days.
- measures to protect against loss and unauthorized alteration.

**4. Procedures for regular review, assessment and evaluation (Art. 32(1)(d) GDPR)**
- Recovery, deletion, tenant separation and role-based access must be tested according to risk; material changes require reassessment. Source-code review does not replace evidence of production configuration.
- a security review of the application was carried out (including hardening of database functions, revocation of PUBLIC/anonymous execution rights); ongoing updates of the components used.
- data protection by design and by default (Art. 25 GDPR): restricted visibility of coworker data by default, resolution of names only via access-restricted functions; ability to pseudonymize/delete staff records as described in Section 15 of the Privacy Policy.
